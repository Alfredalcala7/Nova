Technology in education: the good and the risky

The same tool can help one student and mislead another. Switch between the two sides below.

  • Wider access to learning resourcesLessons, books, and practice material can reach students far from a school library.
  • More independent learningStudents can study at their own pace and revisit topics they find difficult.
  • Immediate feedbackA practice quiz tells you right away what you got wrong, while there is still time to fix it.
  • Better organizationPlanners and reminders keep subjects and deadlines in one place.
  • Self-monitoringProgress records help students notice which topics need more attention.
  • Overreliance on AI answersGetting an answer is not the same as understanding it. Copying skips the practice that builds skill.
  • Inaccurate informationDigital tools, including AI, can be confidently wrong. Check important facts against reliable sources.
  • Unequal accessSome schools lack even electricity, so devices and connectivity are not available to everyone.
  • Too much screen timeLearning benefits can fade when technology is used excessively.
  • Privacy and weak regulationStudent data collected by apps needs protection, and UNESCO has pointed to a lack of proper governance and regulation.
What UNESCO recommends. Technology should be introduced based on evidence that it is appropriate, equitable, scalable, and sustainable, and it should never replace in-person, teacher-led instruction.
Source: UNESCO GEM Report 2023 summary and full report

How to use technology wisely

  • Try first, then check.Attempt the question yourself before looking at any answer.
  • Verify what matters.Confirm facts with a textbook, teacher, or trusted source.
  • Protect your data.Prefer tools that keep your information on your own device.
  • Take breaks from screens.Mix digital study with notes, discussion, and rest.
