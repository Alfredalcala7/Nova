Nova 2.0: how real accounts would work

A plan, not a finished feature. Real accounts need a server, and protecting students' information is a responsibility.

Why the prototype is not real

A web page running only in the browser cannot safely store or check passwords, because anyone using that computer can read or change what the browser saves. It also cannot send email, so it cannot deliver a real 6-digit code. Google and Apple sign-in can only be finished by a server. The account prototype shows the screens and the flow, and it says so on the page.

What a real system needs

  • A backend serverA small web service (for example, Python with FastAPI) that checks passwords, issues sessions, and decides who may see what.
  • A databaseStores accounts and learning data. Passwords are saved only as hashes made with a trusted algorithm, never as readable text.
  • An email serviceSends the 6-digit codes and password reset messages. Reliable delivery usually needs a proper provider and a domain.
  • Google sign-inNeeds a Google Cloud project, a consent screen, and a callback address on our server. Setup is free but takes care.
  • Apple sign-inNeeds a paid Apple developer membership and extra configuration. Check the current price before deciding.
  • Hosting with HTTPSEncrypts everything in transit. Free tiers exist for small projects, but their limits and terms change.

How the 6-digit code and sign-in would be protected

  • Unpredictable codesGenerated with a secure random generator, valid for about 10 minutes, usable once, and stored only as a hash.
  • Limits on guessingA few wrong tries lock the code, and requests for new codes and login attempts are rate limited.
  • Careful messagesErrors say "email or password is incorrect" and never reveal whether an email has an account.
  • Separate reset processPassword reset uses its own expiring, single-use code, not the sign-up code.
  • Server-side checksEvery protected action is checked on the server, and each student can only see their own data.

What information we would ask for, and why

  • Email and password (required)To log in and to send codes.
  • Full name and role (required)To greet the user and to show the right profile questions for students, teachers, or other users.
  • Education details (students and teachers only)Level, strand or course, and year for students; subjects and level for teachers. Used to tailor Nova, and never required of everyone.
  • Gender (optional)Not needed for any Nova feature, so we would leave it optional with a "prefer not to say" choice, or leave it out.
  • Age and birthday (not collected)Nova does not need them, and a birthday already reveals age. Collecting less data means less to protect.
Before collecting real data. Philippine privacy law (the Data Privacy Act of 2012) may apply once real personal information is collected, and extra care is needed for students under 18. Ask your instructor or school first.

Suggested build order

  1. Backend skeleton and database for accounts.
  2. Sign-up and login with server-side password hashing.
  3. Email 6-digit verification.
  4. Password reset.
  5. Profile setup.
  6. Google sign-in, then Apple sign-in.
  7. Connect the Nova desktop app to the same accounts.

Status: not built. Only the screens exist, in the prototype.
