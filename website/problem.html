The problem: learning is hard to organize, and easy to fake

Students have more tools than ever. Many still lack a simple way to plan, practice, and check what they actually know.

The bigger picture

273 million

children, adolescents, and youth were out of school worldwide in 2024, according to UN data for the 2026 SDG report.

Source: UN SDG Report 2026, Goal 4

SDG 4 asks the world for inclusive, equitable, quality education for everyone. That figure shows how far the world still is from that goal.

Technology is often offered as a shortcut, but UNESCO cautions that it is not an automatic fix. Its 2023 education report notes that one in four primary schools has no electricity, and that learning benefits fade when technology is overused or used without qualified teachers.

Source: UNESCO GEM Report 2023 summary

The problem we focus on

Our campaign looks at a smaller, everyday problem inside that picture: students who do have devices and still struggle to study well.

Three things tend to go wrong. Deadlines and subjects are scattered across notes, chats, and memory. Students reread material without testing themselves, so they cannot tell what they really know. And when answers are one search or one AI prompt away, it becomes tempting to collect answers instead of learning them.

A note on evidence. These three issues are our framing of the problem, based on common student experience and the concerns raised in the UNESCO report. We are not claiming that Nova has been proven to improve grades.

Who is affected

  • College and senior high school studentsJuggling several subjects, deadlines, and exams at once.
  • Students with limited accessThose who share devices or have unreliable internet need tools that work offline and cost nothing.
  • Teachers and familiesThey guide how technology is used, and their role does not disappear when a tool is introduced.
