Science, technology, and society, shown through Nova

Each one shapes the others. Here is how that plays out in studying.

The three parts

Science

Research on memory finds that practicing recall helps long-term retention more than rereading. In Roediger and Karpicke's 2006 study, students who tested themselves remembered more after a week than students who simply restudied.

Roediger and Karpicke, 2006

Technology

Nova turns that finding into a tool: practice quizzes with immediate feedback, a planner for deadlines, and progress records that point to topics worth reviewing.

Society

Students, teachers, and schools decide how tools are used. UNESCO stresses access, teacher preparation, and governance, and says technology should never replace teacher-led instruction.

UNESCO GEM Report 2023

It goes both ways

  • Society shapes technologyStudents need free, offline tools, so Nova stores data on the device and needs no account or internet connection.
  • Technology shapes societyEasy answers can change study habits. That is why Nova asks students to write and take their own questions rather than supplying ready-made answers.
  • Science checks bothUNESCO notes that impartial evidence on education technology is limited. We therefore describe Nova as a study aid and make no claim that it improves grades.

The link to SDG 4

SDG 4 aims for inclusive, equitable, quality education. Nova addresses a small part of that goal: helping an individual student organize study and practice more effectively with a free tool.

It does not solve access, funding, or teacher shortages. Those need the policy action described in the UNESCO reports, which is why this campaign pairs the tool with honest information about technology's limits.
